Lecture 7

Below is a density histogram showing the distribution of "Runtime", in minutes, for all 25 films in ghibli.


Problem 2.1

How many Studio Ghibli films are at least 90 minutes long?

Answer: 19

Solution: We know that the entire area of this histogram is equal to 1. Instead of calculating the area of each individual bin that is above 90, we can simply calculate the area of the [70, 80) bin and the [80, 90) bins, sum these two areas, and subtract from 1. This will give us the remaining area, which is equivalent to calculating the combined area of all of the bins above 90. We get the area of the [70, 80) bin by multiplying its height and width: 0.008 * 10 = 0.08. We get the area of the [80, 90) bin by multiplying its height and width: 0.016 * 10 = 0.16. Summing these two areas, we get 0.08 + 0.16 = 0.24. Now, we can subtract from 1, getting 1 - 0.24 = 0.76. Since 0.76 is a proportion, we know that 76% of films in our dataset are at least 90 minutes long. We can calculate our final answer now: 0.76 * 25 = 19.

Problem 2.2

Imagine we changed our histogram to have just one bin, from 70 to 140 minutes. What would the height of this bin be? Give your answer as an exact fraction or decimal.

Answer: \frac{1}{70}

Solution: Recall that the entire area of our histogram must equal 1. If we only have one bin from 70 to 140 which represents all of our data, the area of this bin must equal 1. We also know the width, which is 140 - 70 = 70. Knowing that the equation for finding the area is area = height * width, we can plug in our values to get 1 = height * 70. Solving for height, we get 1 / 70.

Solution: We want to know how many films are of each genre for each director. The use of the word “each” suggests that we want to use groupby here with the attributes Director and Genre. To do this, we pass an array of columns that we want to group by. this is where ["Director", "Genre"] comes from. Now, our DataFrame has one row for every director-genre combination, which is what we want. We want to know how many films are present in each combination. This means that we want to use .count(), which counts the number of films that are of a specific director-genre combination.

Problem 4

Lecture 9

Kate loves Studio Ghibli and she has been recording her viewings of their films in the kate DataFrame, which is shown in full below. Note that kate has repeated rows since Kate watched some films twice.

In ghibli, there are 11 films directed by Hayao Miyazaki and only 2 directed by Hiromasa Yonebayashi.

How many rows are in the DataFrame resulting from ghibli.merge(kate, on="Director")?

Answer: 81

Solution: For every single row (film) that is directed by Hayao Miyazaki in ghibli, it will match with all of the rows (films) in kate that have a Director of “Hayao Miyazaki”. We know that there are 11 rows in ghibli that have a Director attribute of “Hayao Miyazaki”. We know that there are 7 rows in kate that have have the same (Director attribute of “Hayao Miyazaki”). Each of the 11 rows in ghibli will match with all 7 of the rows in kate. With math, we can write this as 11 * 7 = 77. We can now do the same with Hiromasa Yonebayashi films. There are 2 films directed by Hiromasa Yonebayashi in ghibli. In kate, there are 2 films that she has watched that are directed by Hiromasa Yonebayashi. Each of the 2 rows in ghibli will match with all 2 of the rows in kate. With math, we can write this as 2 * 2 = 4. Now, we know the final DataFrame will have 77 + 4 = 81 rows.
