Option 2 and Option 3

A 95% CLT-based confidence interval is approximately \text{sample mean} \pm 2 \cdot \frac{\text{sample SD}}{\sqrt{n}}. The interval [20.0, 22.0] has a midpoint of 21 and a margin of error of 1. Since the sample size is n=400, we have 1 = 2 \cdot \frac{\text{SD}}{\sqrt{400}} = \frac{\text{SD}}{10}. Solving gives \text{SD} = 10.


Problem 9.3

You now want to use the same data to generate another 95% CLT-based confidence interval for a new parameter: the proportion of bands with four or more members. What is the maximum possible width of this new confidence interval? Give your answer as an exact decimal or simplified fraction.

Answer:

0.1

Solution:

For a sample proportion \hat{p}, a 95% CLT-based confidence interval is approximately \hat{p} \pm 2\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}, so its total width is 4\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}. The expression \hat{p}(1-\hat{p}) is maximized when \hat{p}=0.5, giving a maximum value of 0.25. With n=400, the maximum width is 4\sqrt{\frac{0.25}{400}} = 4(0.025) = 0.1.

Solution:

Coldplay’s album count is \frac{10-7}{2}=1.5 standard deviations above the mean. In standard units, the regression line predicts y_{\text{su}}=r\cdot x_{\text{su}}, where r=0.6. Therefore, Coldplay’s predicted top album sale is 0.6(1.5)=\boxed{0.9} standard deviations above the mean.

10

Solution:

The regression line has slope r\frac{\text{SD of }y}{\text{SD of }x}=0.6\frac{S}{2}=0.3S and passes through the point of averages (7,21). Since the line also passes through (0,0), its slope must be \frac{21-0}{7-0}=3. Setting 0.3S=3 gives \boxed{S=10}.

Problem 13.2

Suppose you randomly draw one band uniformly from bands. You are told that this band is not a rock band. What is the conditional probability that the band is active? Give your answer as an exact decimal or simplified fraction.

\frac{7}{31}

Solution:

Since we know the selected band is not a rock band, we restrict our attention to the 310 non-rock bands.
