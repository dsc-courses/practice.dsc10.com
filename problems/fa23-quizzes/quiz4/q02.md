# BEGIN PROB

You want to estimate the proportion of DSC majors who have a Netflix
subscription. To do so, you will survey a random sample of DSC majors
and ask them whether they have a Netflix subscription. You will then
create a 95% confidence interval for the proportion of "yes\" answers in
the population, based on the responses in your sample. You decide that
your confidence interval should have a width of at most 0.10.

# BEGIN SUBPROB

In order for your confidence interval to have a width of at most 0.10,
the standard deviation of the distribution of the sample proportion must
be at most $T$. What is $T$? Give your answer as an exact decimal.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer**: 0.025

By the Central Limit Theorem, the distribution of the sample proportion is roughly normal (similar to a distribution of sample means from a population). In a normal distribution, about 95% of values fall within 2 standard deviations of the mean. So a 95% confidence interval extends about 2 standard deviations on each side of the center, which means

$$\text{width of interval} = 4 \cdot \text{SD of the distribution of the sample proportion}.$$

We want the width to be at most $0.10$, so

$$4 \cdot T = 0.10 \implies T = \frac{0.10}{4} = \boxed{0.025}.$$

# BEGIN SUBPROB

Using the fact that the standard deviation of any dataset of 0s and 1s
is no more than 0.5, calculate the minimum number of people you would
need to survey so that the width of your confidence interval is at most
0.10. Give your answer as an integer.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer**: 400

The standard deviation of the distribution of the sample proportion (a sample mean of 0s and 1s) is

$$\text{SD of distribution of sample proportion} = \frac{\text{SD of the population}}{\sqrt{\text{sample size}}}.$$

Each response is a 0 or a 1, so the population SD is at most $0.5$. We want the confidence interval width to be **at most 0.10**, which requires the SD of the sample proportion to be **at most $0.025$** (from part (a)). Because the interval width is proportional to this SD, setting an upper bound on it caps the width. This gives the inequality:

$$\frac{0.5}{\sqrt{n}} \le 0.025$$

Solving for $n$ gives the minimum sample size:

$$\sqrt{n} \geq \frac{0.5}{0.025} = 20 \implies n \geq 20^2 = \boxed{400}.$$
