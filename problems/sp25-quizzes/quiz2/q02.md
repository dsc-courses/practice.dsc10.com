# BEGIN SUBPROB

How many Studio Ghibli films are at least $90$ minutes long?

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** 19

**Solution:**
We know that the entire area of this histogram is equal to `1`. Instead of calculating the area of each individual bin that is above `90`, we can simply calculate the area of the `[70, 80)` bin and the `[80, 90)` bins, sum these two areas, and subtract from 1. This will give us the remaining area, which is equivalent to calculating the combined area of all of the bins above `90`. We get the area of the `[70, 80)` bin by multiplying its height and width: `0.008 * 10 = 0.08`. We get the area of the `[80, 90)` bin by multiplying its height and width: `0.016 * 10 = 0.16`. Summing these two areas, we get `0.08 + 0.16 = 0.24`. Now, we can subtract from `1`, getting `1 - 0.24 = 0.76`. Since `0.76` is a proportion, we know that 76% of films in our dataset are at least `90` minutes long. We can calculate our final answer now: `0.76 * 25 = 19`.

# BEGIN SUBPROB

Imagine we changed our histogram to have just one bin, from $70$ to
$140$ minutes. What would the height of this bin be? Give your answer as
an exact fraction or decimal.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** $\frac{1}{70}$

**Solution:**
Recall that the entire area of our histogram must equal `1`. If we only have one bin from `70` to `140` which represents **all** of our data, the area of this bin must equal `1`. We also know the width, which is `140 - 70 = 70`. Knowing that the equation for finding the area is `area = height * width`, we can plug in our values to get `1 = height * 70`. Solving for height, we get `1 / 70`.
