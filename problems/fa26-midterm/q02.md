# BEGIN PROB

After a particularly difficult DSC 10 quiz, students flood Campuswire with questions. The course staff scramble to respond, but each new question has only a 40% chance of receiving a response within ten minutes, independently of other questions.

**For all parts, give your answer as a mathematical expression. You do not need to simplify.**

# BEGIN SUBPROB

What is the probability that the first two questions do not receive responses within ten minutes, but the third question does?

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** $0.6^2 \cdot 0.4$

Each question goes unanswered with probability $1 - 0.4 = 0.6$. Since the questions are independent, we multiply: $0.6 \cdot 0.6 \cdot 0.4$.

# BEGIN SUBPROB

What is the probability that exactly two of the first three questions receive a response within ten minutes?

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** $3 \cdot 0.4^2 \cdot 0.6$

Any one ordering, such as response, response, no response, has probability $0.4^2 \cdot 0.6$. The unanswered question could be the first, second, or third, so there are three such orderings, each equally likely. Adding them gives $3 \cdot 0.4^2 \cdot 0.6$.

What is the probability that none of the first three questions receive responses within ten minutes?

**Answer:** $0.6^3$

Each question independently goes unanswered with probability 0.6, so the probability all three go unanswered is $0.6^3$.

# BEGIN SUBPROB

Given that exactly one of the first three questions receives a response within ten minutes, what is the probability that it is the third question?

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** $\frac{1}{3}$

If exactly one question receives a response, it could be the first, second, or third. Each case has probability $0.4 \cdot 0.6^2$, so they're equally likely, and the chance it's the third is $\frac{1}{3}$. Equivalently:

$$P(\text{only the 3rd} \mid \text{exactly one}) = \frac{0.6^2 \cdot 0.4}{3 \cdot 0.4 \cdot 0.6^2} = \frac{1}{3}$$

# BEGIN SUBPROB

Let $a$, $b$, $c$, and $d$ represent the correct answers to parts (a), (b), (c), and (d), respectively.

Given that at least one of the first three questions receives a response within ten minutes, what is the probability that the third question is the only one to receive a response within ten minutes?

( ) $\dfrac{d}{a+b}$
( ) $\dfrac{a}{1-c}$
( ) $\dfrac{d}{1-c}$
( ) $\dfrac{a}{a+b}$

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** $\dfrac{a}{1-c}$

Let $A$ be "only the third question receives a response" and $B$ be "at least one question receives a response." Then

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \text{ and } B)}{P(B)}$$

If $A$ happens, $B$ must too, so the numerator is $P(A) = a$. "At least one" is the complement of "none," so the denominator is $1 - c$.
