# BEGIN PROB

Consider the following pair of hypotheses:

-   **Null:** The proportion of `"Comedy"` movies with an average rating
    above 9 **equals** the proportion of `"Action"` movies with a rating
    above 9.

-   **Alternative:** The proportion of `"Comedy"` movies with an average
    rating above 9 is **greater than** the proportion of `"Action"`
    movies with a rating above 9.

Let $C_9$ be the proportion of `"Comedy"` movies with a rating above 9
and let $A_9$ be the proportion of `"Action"` movies with a rating above
9. Which of the following are valid test statistics to test these
hypotheses? **Select all that apply.**

[ ] $C_9$ - 0.5
[ ] $A_9$ - 0.5
[ ] $A_9 \cdot C_9$
[ ] $A_9 + C_9$
[ ] $C_9 - A_9$
[ ] $A_9 - C_9$
[ ] $|A_9 - C_9|$
[ ] $|C_9 - A_9|$

# BEGIN SOLUTION
**Answer**: $C_9 - A_9$ and $A_9 - C_9$

The hypotheses compare two proportions, and the alternative has a direction ("Comedy is **greater**"). So a valid test statistic needs to do two things:

1. **Compare $C_9$ and $A_9$ to each other.** $C_9 - 0.5$ and $A_9 - 0.5$ each look at only one proportion. $A_9 \cdot C_9$ and $A_9 + C_9$ use both, but they don't measure how far apart the two are. For example, $(0.3, 0.5)$ and $(0.5, 0.3)$ have the same sum and the same product.
2. **Tell us which proportion is bigger.** $|A_9 - C_9|$ and $|C_9 - A_9|$ measure how far apart the two proportions are, but they can't tell whether Comedy or Action is higher. They'd fit a two-sided alternative ("the proportions are different"), not this one.

$C_9 - A_9$ and $A_9 - C_9$ satisfy both. With $C_9 - A_9$, large positive values point toward the alternative. With $A_9 - C_9$, large negative values do. Either one works, as long as we calculate the p-value in the matching direction.
