# BEGIN PROB
A __(a)__ provides insight into how much a __(b)__ might vary across samples and is key for estimating the corresponding __(c)__. This type of distribution is an example of a __(d)__ because it is based on trials of an experiment, as opposed to a __(e)__, which is based on theory.


The **five terms that should fill in the blanks are parameter,statistic, probability distribution, empirical distribution, bootstrap distribution**. Determine which word corresponds with each letter blank. 


# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer (a):** bootstrapped distribution

**Answer (b):** statistic 

**Answer (c):** paramter

**Answer (d):** empirical distribution 

**Answer (e):** probability distribution 


| Blank | Answer | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (a) <br><br>| **Bootstrapped distribution** <br><br>| Repeatedly resample the original sample with replacement, and find a statistic for each resample. The spread of the resulting statistics estimates how much the statistic would vary across fresh samples. <br><br>|
| (b) <br><br>| **Statistic** <br><br>| A number computed from a sample, such as the mean visitor count over 50 sampled days, can change when we draw a new sample. <br><br>|
| (c) <br><br>| **Parameter** <br><br>| The mean visitor count overall (not just 50 days) is a fixed population quantity. The sample mean estimates it. <br><br>|
| (d) <br><br>| **Empirical distribution** <br><br>| The frequencies in the bootstrap distribution come from the resampling trials we actually ran. <br><br>|
| (e) | **Probability distribution** | For example, the normal curve is a theoretical probability estimation of a normal distribution. |
