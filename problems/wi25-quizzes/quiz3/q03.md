# BEGIN PROB

Your friend at SDSU records the number of students who visit their library each day, for $100$ days. They tell you that the average is
$6{,}000$ and that the standard deviation is $500$.

# BEGIN SUBPROB

Without knowing anything about the distribution of your friend's data, find the endpoints of the smallest interval which is guaranteed to
contain at least $75\%$ of your friend's data. Both endpoints should be
given as **integers**.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** $[5000, 7000]$

Chebyshev’s inequality guarantees that at least $1-1/k^2$ of the observations lie within $k$ standard deviations of the mean. To guarantee 75%, the smallest $k$ given by this bound satisfies

$$
1-\frac{1}{k^2}=0.75 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k^2=4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=2.
$$

The daily counts have mean 6,000 and standard deviation 500. Moving two standard deviations in each direction gives the interval

$$
[6{,}000-2(500),\;6{,}000+2(500)] = [5{,}000,\;7{,}000].
$$

At least **75 of the 100 recorded days** had between **5,000 and 7,000 visitors**.

If you then learn that your friend's data is normally distributed, approximately what percentage of the data is actually contained in the interval you found above? Give your answer as an **integer**.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** $95\%$

The endpoints from 3(a) are exactly two standard deviations below and above the mean. So, approximately 95% of normally distributed daily counts fall in that range.
