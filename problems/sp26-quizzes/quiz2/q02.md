# BEGIN PROB

Assume you also have access to another DataFrame, `bonus`, shown in full
below. Determine the number of rows in
`cards.merge(bonus, on="arena_unlocked")`.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer**: $7$

Explanation: The expression `cards.merge(bonus, on="arena_unlocked")` merges the two DataFrames using the common "arena_unlocked" column. By default, `.merge()` performs an inner join, meaning only rows with matching "arena_unlocked" values in both DataFrames appear in the result.

The bonus DataFrame contains three rows: two with "arena_unlocked" equal to $1$ and one with "arena_unlocked" equal to $2$. From the displayed rows of cards, we can see that two cards are unlocked in Arena 1 and three cards are unlocked in Arena 2.

When merging, each row in one DataFrame is matched with every row in the other DataFrame that has the same value in the merging column.

For Arena 1, there are two matching rows in cards and two in bonus. Each of the two cards matches both rows in bonus, producing $2 * 2 = 4$ rows.

For Arena 2, there are three matching rows in cards and one in bonus. Each of the three cards matches that single row, producing $3 * 1 = 3$ rows.

The bonus DataFrame contains no other arena values, so cards unlocked in other arenas will not appear in the merged DataFrame.

Therefore, the total number of rows is:

$(2 * 2) + (3 * 1) = 4 + 3 = 7$
