Using the density histogram above, how many cards have an\
`"arena_unlocked"` that is **strictly less than 6**?

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer**: $24$

Explanation: In a density histogram, the area of each bar represents the proportion of observations in that bin. To find how many cards have an "arena_unlocked" value strictly less than $6$, we calculate the total area of the bars covering values below $6$. The first bar has width $4 - 1 = 3$ and height $0.10$, giving an area of $0.30$. The second bar has width $6 - 4 = 2$ and height $0.15$, giving an area of $0.30$. Adding these areas gives $0.30 + 0.30 = 0.60$, meaning $60$% of the cards have an "arena_unlocked" value less than $6$. Since the DataFrame contains $40$ cards, the number of such cards is $0.60 * 40 = 24$.

Give a **mathematical expression** that evaluates to the exact height of
the rightmost bin in the histogram above. You do not need to simplify.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer**: $\frac{1 - \left(0.10\cdot(4-1) + 0.15\cdot(6-4) + 0.08\cdot(9-6)\right)}{15 - 9} = \frac{0.16}{6} = \frac{0.08}{3}$

Explanation: The total area of a density histogram must equal $1$. To determine the height of the rightmost bar, we first calculate the areas of the other three bars. Their areas are $0.10 * 3 = 0.30$, $0.15 * 2 = 0.30$, and $0.08 * 3 = 0.24$. Adding them gives $0.84$. Therefore, the remaining area for the rightmost bar is $1 - 0.84 = 0.16$. The rightmost bar extends from $9$ to $15$, so its width is $15 - 9 = 6$. Since a bar's area equals its width multiplied by its height, we divide the remaining area by the width to obtain the height: $0.16 / 6 = 0.08 / 3$.
