# BEGIN PROB

Which of the following quantities must be known in order to construct a
CLT-based confidence interval for the population mean? Select all that
apply.

[ ] Shape of the population (normal or not)
[ ] Shape of the sample (normal or not)
[ ] Mean of the population
[ ] Mean of the sample
[ ] Standard deviation of the population
[ ] Standard deviation of the sample
[ ] Size of the population
[ ] Size of the sample

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer**: Mean of the sample, Standard deviation of the sample, Size of the sample

A 95% CLT-based confidence interval for the population mean is

$$\left[\text{sample mean} - 2 \cdot \frac{\text{sample SD}}{\sqrt{\text{sample size}}}, \ \text{sample mean} + 2 \cdot \frac{\text{sample SD}}{\sqrt{\text{sample size}}}\right]$$

So the only things we need are the sample mean, the sample SD, and the sample size.

- **Shape of the population or sample**: The CLT says the distribution of the sample mean is roughly normal for large samples, **no matter** what the population looks like. We don't need either one to be normal.
- **Mean of the population**: This is the parameter we're trying to estimate. If we knew it, we wouldn't need a confidence interval.
- **SD of the population**: We usually don't know it, so we use the sample SD as an estimate.
- **Size of the population**: This doesn't appear in the formula.
