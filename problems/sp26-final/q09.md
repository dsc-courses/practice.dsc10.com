You want to use the data in `bands` to create a 95% CLT-based confidence interval for the mean `"top_album_sales"` of all bands in the population. The data in `bands` was drawn **without replacement** but the CLT is a statement about samples drawn with replacement. What makes it reasonable to still use the CLT despite this discrepancy?

( ) The sample standard deviation is a good approximation of the population standard deviation.
( ) The statistic of interest is a sample mean.
( ) The population distribution of `"top_album_sales"` is likely normal.
( ) The sample is much smaller than the population.
( ) The CLT is less computational than bootstrapping, so we do not need to resample with replacement.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:**

Option 4

**Solution:**

The CLT is typically applied to independent observations, whereas sampling without replacement introduces dependence between observations. However, when the sample is much smaller than the population, removing one observation has very little effect on the distribution of the remaining observations. Therefore, sampling without replacement behaves approximately like sampling with replacement, making the CLT a reasonable approximation. The other options do not address the dependence introduced by sampling without replacement.

Your 95% CLT-based confidence interval comes out to be $[20.0,\ 22.0]$. Based on this information, estimate `np.std(bands.get("top_album_sales"))` to the **nearest integer**.

::: center
:::

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:**

10

**Solution:**

A 95% CLT-based confidence interval is approximately $\text{sample mean} \pm 2 \cdot \frac{\text{sample SD}}{\sqrt{n}}$. The interval $[20.0, 22.0]$ has a midpoint of 21 and a margin of error of 1. Since the sample size is $n=400$, we have $1 = 2 \cdot \frac{\text{SD}}{\sqrt{400}} = \frac{\text{SD}}{10}$. Solving gives $\text{SD} = 10$.

You now want to use the same data to generate another 95% CLT-based confidence interval for a new parameter: the proportion of bands with four or more members. What is the **maximum possible width** of this new confidence interval? Give your answer as an **exact decimal or simplified fraction**.

::: center
:::

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:**

0.1

**Solution:**

For a sample proportion $\hat{p}$, a 95% CLT-based confidence interval is approximately $\hat{p} \pm 2\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}$, so its total width is $4\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}$. The expression $\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})$ is maximized when $\hat{p}=0.5$, giving a maximum value of $0.25$. With $n=400$, the maximum width is $4\sqrt{\frac{0.25}{400}} = 4(0.025) = 0.1$.
