::: center
|          | Rock | Pop |
|:---------|-----:|----:|
| Michelle | $4$  | $3$ |
| Pranav   | $2$  | $4$ |
| Kate     | $x$  | $y$ |
:::

For each tutor, we created a ten-row DataFrame containing just their top ten bands. Each was queried directly from `bands`, so each tutor's DataFrame has the same columns as `bands`, just with fewer rows.

# BEGIN SUBPROB

Suppose we merge Michelle's ten-row DataFrame with Pranav's ten-row DataFrame, on the column `"genre"`. What are the minimum and maximum possible number of rows in the result?

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:**

- **Min:** 20
- **Max:** 32

**Solution:**

When merging on `"genre"`, each genre contributes the product of its row counts in the two DataFrames, so Rock contributes $4 \cdot 2 = 8$ rows and Pop contributes $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ rows, for a guaranteed total of **20 rows**. Michelle has 3 remaining bands and Pranav has 4 remaining bands, all belonging to genres other than Rock or Pop. The **minimum** occurs when these remaining bands have no genres in common, giving 20 rows. The **maximum** occurs when all remaining bands share the same third genre, producing an additional $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ rows, for a total of **32 rows**.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:**

- **Min:** 0
- **Max:** 8

**Solution:**

When merging on `"band"`, each matching band appears once in the result, since each tutor's top ten list contains distinct bands. The **minimum** is 0 if Michelle and Pranav have no bands in common. For the **maximum**, they can share at most 2 Rock bands, since Pranav has only 2, and at most 3 Pop bands, since Michelle has only 3. They can also share at most 3 bands from other genres, since Michelle has 3 remaining bands and Pranav has 4. Therefore, the maximum number of matching bands is $2 + 3 + 3 = \mathbf{8}$.

Finally, suppose we merge Michelle's ten-row DataFrame with Kate's ten-row DataFrame, on the column `"genre"`. What are the minimum and maximum possible number of rows in the result? Give your answers as **unsimplified algebraic expressions** involving $x$ and $y$.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:**

- **Min:** $4x + 3y$
- **Max:** $4x + 3y + 3(10 - x - y)$

**Solution:**

Merging on `"genre"` guarantees $4x$ matching rows for Rock and $3y$ matching rows for Pop, giving a **minimum of $4x + 3y$** if Michelle's and Kate's remaining bands have no other genres in common. Michelle has $10 - 4 - 3 = 3$ bands from other genres, while Kate has $10 - x - y$ such bands. The **maximum** occurs when all of these remaining bands share the same genre, creating an additional $3(10 - x - y)$ matching rows. Therefore, the maximum is **$4x + 3y + 3(10 - x - y)$**.
