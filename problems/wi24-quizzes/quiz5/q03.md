# BEGIN PROB

Charlie spends 100 days in each of three California cities (San Diego,
San Francisco, and Los Angeles). He records the primary weather for each
day in each location. The table below shows these values as proportions.
For example, 43 of the 100 days that Charlie spent in San Diego had
primarily sunny weather.

::: center
  **Weather**          **San Diego**   **San Francisco**   **Los Angeles**  
  ------------------- --------------- ------------------- ----------------- --
  **sunny**                0.43              0.51               0.34        
  **partly cloudy**        0.24              0.18               0.22        
  **cloudy**               0.22              0.15               0.26        
  **rainy**                0.11              0.16               0.18        
:::

# BEGIN SUBPROB

What is the total variation distance between the San Diego and San
Francisco weather distributions? Give your answer as an **exact
decimal**.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** 0.13

To find the total variation distance (TVD), we take the absolute difference between the two distributions in each category, add them up, and divide by 2.

The absolute differences between San Diego and San Francisco are $|0.43 - 0.51| = 0.08$ for sunny, $|0.24 - 0.18| = 0.06$ for partly cloudy, $|0.22 - 0.15| = 0.07$ for cloudy, and $|0.11 - 0.16| = 0.05$ for rainy.

Adding these gives $0.08 + 0.06 + 0.07 + 0.05 = 0.26$, and dividing by 2 gives a TVD of $\frac{0.26}{2} = 0.13$.

# BEGIN SUBPROB

Charlie reconsiders his weather categories and decides to count any
"partly cloudy" days as "cloudy" days, thereby combining these two
categories into one. If he recalculates the TVD between any pair of
cities, what will he find?

( ) The new TVD is less than or equal to the old TVD.
( ) The new TVD is greater than or equal to the old TVD.
( ) The new TVD is equal to the old TVD.
( ) There is not enough information to determine the relationship
between the new\
TVD and the old TVD.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** The new TVD is less than or equal to the old TVD.

When we combine "partly cloudy" and "cloudy" into one category, the two differences in those categories get added together **before** we take the absolute value. If the two differences have the same sign, combining them doesn't change anything, and the TVD stays the same. If they have opposite signs, they partially cancel out, and the TVD gets smaller. Combining categories can never make the TVD larger.

For example, between San Diego and San Francisco, San Diego has more partly cloudy days ($0.24$ vs. $0.18$) **and** more cloudy days ($0.22$ vs. $0.15$). Both differences go in the same direction, so the combined category has an absolute difference of $|0.46 - 0.33| = 0.13$, which is the same as the old $0.06 + 0.07 = 0.13$. The TVD stays the same.

Between San Diego and Los Angeles, San Diego has more partly cloudy days ($0.24$ vs. $0.22$) but fewer cloudy days ($0.22$ vs. $0.26$). The differences go in opposite directions, so the combined category has an absolute difference of $|0.46 - 0.48| = 0.02$, which is smaller than the old $0.02 + 0.04 = 0.06$. The TVD goes down.

Since the TVD can stay the same for some pairs and go down for others, the new TVD is less than or equal to the old TVD.
