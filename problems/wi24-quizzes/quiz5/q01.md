Suppose you want to estimate the proportion of UCSD students that prefer
sunny days over rainy days. You plan to survey 900 students, then
construct a 95% confidence interval for this proportion. What is the
**widest possible width** for the resulting confidence interval? Give
your answer as a **fully simplified fraction**.

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** $\frac{1}{15}$

We can think of each student's response as a 1 (prefers sunny days) or a 0 (prefers rainy days). The proportion of students who prefer sunny days is then the mean of these 0s and 1s, so we can use the CLT to build a confidence interval for it.

A 95% confidence interval goes 2 SDs of the sample mean in each direction from the sample mean, so its width is $4 \cdot \frac{\text{sample SD}}{\sqrt{\text{sample size}}}$. To make the width as large as possible, we need the sample SD to be as large as possible.

For a collection of 0s and 1s, the SD is largest when there are equally many 0s and 1s, and in that case the SD is $0.5$. So the widest possible width is $4 \cdot \frac{0.5}{\sqrt{900}} = \frac{2}{30} = \frac{1}{15}$.

# BEGIN SUBPROB

If you decide to survey 450 students instead of 900 students for your
sample, the maximum possible width of your 95% confidence interval
would:

( ) double
( ) increase by more than double
( ) increase by less than double

# BEGIN SOLUTION

**Answer:** increase by less than double

The sample size is under a square root in the formula for the width, $4 \cdot \frac{0.5}{\sqrt{\text{sample size}}}$. Cutting the sample size in half doesn't double the width. It multiplies the width by $\sqrt{2}$.

With 450 students, the widest possible width is $4 \cdot \frac{0.5}{\sqrt{450}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{450}}$. Compared to the width with 900 students, this is $\frac{\sqrt{900}}{\sqrt{450}} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$ times as large. Since $1.41$ is less than $2$, the width increases by less than double.
